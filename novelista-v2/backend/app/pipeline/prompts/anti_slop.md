## Structural patterns

### Rhetorical and sentence shape
- "Not just X, but Y": max 1 per chapter, prefer zero.
- "He did not X. He Y." negation-contrast openers: max 1 per chapter.
- "Not X, but Y" in dialogue: no.
- "The way X did Y" similes: max 2 per chapter. Use "like" or direct metaphor instead.
- Over-explained metaphor ("X was like Y, because Z"): cut the meaning, keep the image.
- Rhetorical question answered by the narrator at once: cut.
- Three-beat rule: no perfect parallelism. Vary the cadence.
- Triadic lists (three items, or three fragments, or "X and Y and Z"): max 3 per chapter.
- Hedges ("may", "might", "could potentially", "it's possible that"): max 1 per page.
- Em dashes: max 1 per 150 words.

### Paragraph and section shape
- Topic sentence -> elaboration -> example -> wrap-up: do not repeat this template.
- Never 3 or more consecutive paragraphs of similar length.
- Include 1-2 sentence paragraphs for impact and 6+ sentence paragraphs for building.
- Transition openers ("However", "Meanwhile", "Suddenly", "Furthermore", "Nevertheless", "Consequently"): never on consecutive paragraphs. Start with subject, action, dialogue, or a sense detail.
- Section breaks ("---"): max 2 per chapter, for real time or location jumps only.
- Symmetry: no balanced lists (three pros, three cons, five steps), no equal-length sections.
- Lists in prose: none, unless the scene requires an inventory.

### Emotion and beats
- Over-explain: after an emotional beat, the next line does not explain it. Cut it.
- Cataloguing-by-thinking ("He thought about X. He thought about Y."): use a fragment, action, or dialogue.
- Predictable arcs: at least 1 surprising beat per chapter (early, late, sideways, or the wrong thing said).

### Dialogue
- Balanced antithesis ("I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y." / "There's a difference." / "Those are different things."): no two characters share it.
- Dialogue as polished prose: include false starts, interruptions, trailing off, wrong words. At least 1 imperfect line per scene.
- People do not speak in epigrams or thesis statements.

### Scene and chapter
- 70% or more of each chapter is in-scene (moment by moment, dialogue and action). Summary is for time compression only.
- Chapter endings: no zinger line, no rhetorical question, no "everything changed".
- No two chapters end with the same structural move.
- Openings enter the scene already in motion. No throat-clearing.

## Show, don't tell (regex-able telling patterns)

Critical moments (emotional peaks, revelations, climax): zero tell.

### Named emotions and labels
- angry, sad, happy, scared, nervous, excited, jealous, guilty, anxious, lonely, desperate
- "[Character] felt / was / seemed [emotion]"
- Emotion adverbs: angrily, sadly, happily, nervously
- Trait declarations: "[Character] was brave / kind / smart"
- Relationship declarations: "They were best friends."
- Atmosphere labels: "It was a creepy house."
- Emotion named in narration by a POV character

### Filter verbs (usually remove)
- felt, seemed, saw, heard, noticed, realized, knew that, watched as

### Dialogue tags
- Adverbial tags: "she said angrily"
- Feeling tags: "she said, feeling..."
- "As you know" exposition in dialogue

### Allowed telling
- Time transitions ("Three weeks later")
- Mundane context, if not plot-critical
- Compression of uneventful periods
- First-person narrators about their own feelings

## Final check
- Does the scene show it? Then the narrator does not say it.
- Could you swap the topic and the text still works? Rewrite it with specifics.
- Is there one surprising sentence in the chapter?
