## Chapter 2: The Rust and the Knife

Kael watched the farmer’s back like a wolf watching a wounded stag.

The stag walked with a limp, but the limp was a lie. Under the coarse wool of Elias’s tunic, the air shimmered. Heat waves rippled off the man’s spine, distorting the gray light of the valley. The smell hit Kael first. Not sweat. Not rot. The sharp, metallic tang of ozone, like the taste of a battery held against the tongue. It was the smell of power. Real power. The kind that didn’t come from begging the dirt gods for a few more potatoes.

Kael leaned against the rotting timber of the gatehouse, his fingers tracing the groove of the rusted pipe he kept tucked into his belt. He didn’t move. He just watched the village square fill up.

They came from the huts, the barns, the holes in the earth. Gaunt faces. Eyes wide and wet. They didn’t cheer. They didn’t pray. They smelled the heat radiating from Elias and their pupils dilated. Greed is a quiet thing. It doesn’t shout. It just opens the mouth.

Elias stopped in the center of the square. The cobblestones hissed where his boots touched them. He pulled the tunic down, exposing the ring.

It was silver. Smooth. Cold to the eye, even as it drank the warmth from the air around it. It spun in his palm, a lazy, hypnotic circle.

The villagers took a step forward. Then another. A wall of dirty wool and bare feet.

Then the copper wires sang.

It was a high, thin whine, like a insect trapped in a jar. Kael’s head snapped to the right. Mordecai stood on the steps of the shrine, his robes heavy with woven strands of copper and rusted iron. The wires in his collar vibrated, snapping against his throat. The High Priest’s face was gray, the skin pulled tight over his cheekbones like parchment over bone.

Mordecai didn’t look at the ring with wonder. He looked at it like a man looking at a grenade with the pin pulled.

He descended the steps. His boots made no sound on the stone. He stopped ten paces from Elias. The heat radiating from the farmer pushed against Mordecai’s face, but the Priest didn’t blink.

"You hold a wound in the sky, boy," Mordecai said. His voice was low, rhythmic, but it carried. It wasn’t a shout. It was a diagnosis. "Drop it."

Elias didn’t look up. He stared at the ring. "It hums in the bone, Priest. You feel that? That’s life. That’s the God-sweat."

"It is a drain," Mordecai said. He took a step closer. The copper wires in his robe screamed louder, a discordant shriek that made Kael’s teeth ache. "The drift is wrong. The core is screaming. Put it down before the air catches fire."

Kael spat on the ground. The saliva froze before it hit the dirt.

*Pretty,* Kael thought. *So pretty.*

He pushed off the gatehouse. He didn’t run. Running was for prey. He walked. His boots crunched on the frost that was already creeping out from under Elias’s feet. The crowd parted. They were afraid of the heat, but they were more afraid of Kael. Kael was the knife. Kael was the thing that broke fingers when the bread ran out.

Kael reached the circle of heat. The temperature jumped twenty degrees in two steps. His skin prickled. Sweat popped out on his forehead, then evaporated instantly.

Elias finally looked up. His eyes were bloodshot, the pupils blown wide. He looked drunk. He looked divine.

"Go away, Kael," Elias said. His voice was thick. "You don’t understand. It’s warm. It’s keeping me warm."

"Let me see it," Kael said. He smiled. He let the smile show all his teeth. "Pretty little sun-catcher. Bet it screams when you squeeze it. Let’s see."

Elias pulled the ring back, clutching it to his chest. "Get back."

Kael laughed. It was a wet, guttural sound, like stones grinding in a throat. "Heh. You think that toy stops me?"

Kael drew the pipe. It was a length of scavenged plumbing, jagged at one end, caked in decades of oxidation. He swung it in a tight arc, aiming for Elias’s wrist. A clean break. Take the ring. Take the power.

The pipe hit the air three inches from Elias’s skin.

There was a *crack*. Not the sound of metal on flesh. The sound of physics breaking.

The pipe bent.

It didn’t just dent. It curled like warm taffy. The iron groaned, twisted, and folded back on itself, wrapping around Kael’s own fist. The force threw him backward. He hit the ground hard, the wind knocked out of him. He gasped, choking on the sudden vacuum.

Elias didn’t even flinch. He just held the ring higher. "Stay back," he whispered.

Kael rolled to his feet. His hand was numb. The pipe lay on the stones, bent into a useless pretzel. He looked at his palm. The skin was red, blistered. Not from heat. From the pressure. The air itself had become a wall.

"Rust," Mordecai warned, his voice trembling now. "The resonance is shifting. Elias, drop it! The containment is failing!"

"I am the containment!" Elias shouted. He spun the ring. The light intensified. The villagers screamed, not in fear, but in ecstasy. They reached out, their hands clawing at the air, trying to touch the heat.

Kael saw it. He saw the gap.

Elias was distracted by the crowd. By the worship. He wasn’t watching the shadows.

Kael didn’t go for the ring. He went for the man.

He lunged, low and fast, driving his shoulder into Elias’s gut. The impact should have doubled the farmer over. Instead, Kael felt like he’d slammed into a concrete pillar. The air around Elias was dense, solid. Kael’s shoulder bruised instantly. The bones in his collarbone ground together.

But momentum is a cruel mistress. Elias staggered. The grip loosened.

The ring slipped.

It didn’t fall. It hovered.

It hung in the air, six inches from the cobblestones, spinning faster. The whine became a shriek. The light turned from white to a violent, sickly violet.

Frost exploded outward from the ring.

It wasn’t slow. It was a shockwave of ice. It raced across the square, turning the mud to glass, the air to needles.

Kael felt it hit his legs first. The cold was absolute. It didn’t just freeze the skin; it froze the blood in the veins. His boots stiffened, locking his ankles. He tried to step back, but his legs were pillars of ice.

*Plant 3.*

The ring hovered, spinning faster, the violet light pulsing like a heartbeat. The air around it crystallized, hanging in the air like suspended diamonds. The cold bit through Kael’s trousers, through his skin, into the muscle. It felt like being flayed alive with ice.

Then the weaver stepped in.

Elara moved through the crowd like a ghost. She didn’t look at the ring. She didn’t look at Elias. She looked at the sky.

She raised her hand. Her fingers were long, stained with indigo and blood. She pointed.

The clouds above the valley were not moving with the wind. They were swirling. A perfect, unnatural vortex, spinning clockwise, pulling the gray mist into a single, dark point. The light from that point was too bright. It burned the eyes.

Elara signed. Her hands moved sharp, abrupt. *Stop. Run. Cold. Fire.*

Kael tried to look up. His neck muscles locked. The cold had climbed his thighs, past his hips, wrapping around his ribs. His breath came out in jagged shards of ice that clattered on the stones.

Elara’s eyes met his. There was no fear in them. Only pity. And anger.

Kael backhanded her.

He couldn’t reach her with the ring—his arms were lead, frozen to his sides—but he had his other hand. He swung a fist, fueled by the last dregs of his body heat.

His knuckles connected with her jaw. The crack was louder than the pipe. Elara flew backward, hitting the mud, sliding into the gutter. Blood pooled on the white frost, bright and terrifying.

Kael laughed. It was a weak, wheezing sound. "Pretty," he gasped. "Look at her shake."

He turned back to the ring.

The cold was in his chest now. His heart beat slow, heavy, like a hammer on an anvil. *Thump... thump... thump...*

The ring was calling him.

Not with warmth. With gravity.

It felt like a hook in his navel, pulling him down. The air around the ring was so dense it felt like he was breathing syrup. The ring wanted to be held. It wanted to be *held*.

Kael dragged his frozen legs forward. One step. The ice cracked. Another step. His vision blurred at the edges. The world was turning gray.

He fell to his knees in front of the hovering ring.

Elias was still standing there, holding his arms out, the heat radiating from him keeping the immediate circle of frost at bay. He looked down at Kael with confusion. "Why are you on the ground?"

"Give it," Kael croaked. "Give it to me."

"I can't," Elias said. His voice was slurred. "It’s... it’s too heavy."

"Liar," Kael spat. Ice crystals formed on his lips. He reached out with his left hand. The fingers were blue, stiff. He grabbed the ring.

The moment his skin touched the silver, the hook pulled.

It wasn’t pain. It was emptiness.

The heat in his body didn’t just leave; it was ripped out. The thermal energy was siphoned instantly, violently. His blood turned to slush in his veins. His lungs froze shut.

He screamed, but the sound was sucked out of the air, replaced by the crackle of freezing water.

The temperature in the square dropped forty degrees in seconds.

The villagers around them fell. Not all at once. One by one. A woman dropped her basket of potatoes, her face freezing into a mask of surprise. A child curled up, shivering, his cries turning to whimpers, then silence.

Kael tried to let go. He tried to pull his hand away.

He couldn’t.

The ring was fused to his palm. The gravity well held him there, pinned to the spot, pinned to the object. The cold raced up his arm, past his elbow, into his shoulder. His left arm went numb. Then his right.

Mordecai was on the ground, his copper robes frozen stiff, the wires brittle. He looked up at Kael, his eyes wide, his mouth moving silently. *It’s not a god,* the Priest’s eyes said. *It’s a bomb.*

Kael couldn’t hear him. He couldn’t feel him. He could only feel the cold.

It was deep. It was inside his skull. It was in the marrow of his bones.

He looked at the ring. It was spinning faster now. The violet light was blinding. The air around it was crystallizing into a solid dome.

Kael tried to laugh. He wanted to laugh. He had won. He had the power.

But his lips wouldn’t move. The muscles were frozen solid.

The sky above the vortex tore open.

A line of white fire appeared. Thin at first. Then thick. Then a pillar of pure, blinding light descended from the heavens.

Kael’s eye followed it.

The air screamed.

Then the world went white.
