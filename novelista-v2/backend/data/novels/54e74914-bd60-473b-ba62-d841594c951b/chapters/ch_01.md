## Chapter 1: The Cold Iron

The plowshare bit into the earth and caught.

Elias threw his weight forward, muscles in his shoulders knitting tight like old rope. The iron resisted. Not the soft give of frozen mud, but the hard, unyielding *clink* of stone. He grunted, spittle freezing on his lip, and drove his boot down. The wood of the handle groaned. He leaned until his vision swam, until the gray sky tilted on its axis and the black soil rushed up to meet his face.

Then it gave way.

Not with a crack. With a hum.

It started in the soles of his feet, a vibration that traveled up his shin, rattled his teeth, and settled deep in the marrow of his femur. The plow shuddered. Elias stumbled back, letting the handle slip from his sweat-slicked grip. The tool stood upright for a moment, trembling like a dying animal, then slammed into the dirt.

Silence returned. Heavy. Wet.

Elias stared at the furrow. The black dirt was churned, steaming slightly in the cold air. He wiped his nose on the back of a glove that was more hole than wool. His hands were cracked maps of his own starvation, the fissures weeping red beads that froze before they could fall. He knelt. The mud sucked at his knees, cold enough to burn.

He reached into the furrow.

His fingers closed around metal. But not the rough, pitted iron of the plow. This was smooth. Cold. And heavy.

He pulled.

The object slid free from the earth with a sound like a sigh. Elias sat back on his heels, blinking through the fog that clung to the valley floor. In his palm lay a ring.

It was silver, but not the dull gray of tin or the yellow glare of scavenged gold. It was bright, sharp, and seemed to drink the weak light of the overcast sky. It was about the width of his thumb, toroidal, perfect. No seams. No rust. No pitting from three hundred years of decay.

It spun.

Slowly at first, then faster, a blur of silver light. It didn’t roll. It hovered an inch above his skin, suspended in nothing.

Elias stopped breathing.

The cold that had been gnawing at his ribs since dawn—the same cold that had taken his father, his mother, his little sister in the Winter of Ash—vanished. It didn’t fade. It was ripped away, replaced by a surge of heat that felt violent. It rushed through his veins, turning his blood to water, his bones to fire. He gasped, his chest heaving. The air in his lungs tasted sweet, like ozone and blood.

He looked down at his hands. The cracks in his knuckles were closing. The red weeping stopped. The skin smoothed over, pink and new.

*Hold on,* a voice whispered. Not in his ears. In his head. Behind his eyes. *You are safe.*

Elias laughed. It was a dry, barking sound that hurt his throat. He squeezed the ring. It didn’t move. It stayed suspended in his grip, spinning faster, the hum growing louder, a low thrum that vibrated in his molars.

He stood up.

The world tilted. The ground felt further away. He felt taller. He felt heavier. The hollow ache in his stomach was gone, filled by the weight of the ring. He felt solid. Real. For the first time in ten years, Elias felt like he owned the ground beneath his feet.

He looked at his plow. The iron share was dull, scarred from years of fighting rocks. He looked at the ring. It was perfect.

"God-sweat," he whispered. The words felt clumsy in his mouth. He spat them out. "No. No, that’s too pretty. That’s... that’s the meat of the sky."

He wrapped his apron around the ring. The fabric didn’t burn. It warmed instantly, the wool glowing with a faint, amber light. He tucked the bundle against his chest. The heat seeped through his tunic, into his skin, into his core. He closed his eyes and let out a long, shuddering breath.

A shadow fell over him.

Elias opened one eye.

A crow sat on the ridge above him. The bird was huge, its feathers blacker than the soil. It didn’t caw. It didn’t move. It just stared. Its head twitched, jerking in a sharp, mechanical motion. Its eyes were not the dull black of a bird’s eye. They were white. Blank. Rolling back in their sockets.

Then the crow dropped.

It fell straight down, stiff as a stone. It hit the ground ten feet from Elias with a wet *thud*. No flutter. No挣扎. Just dead weight.

Elias watched the bird twitch once, then still.

He looked up at the ridge. The fog swirled, hiding the rest of the sky. He felt no fear. Only a cold, hard certainty. The bird was a sign. The sky had sent the bird. The sky was watching.

He touched the bundle against his chest. It pulsed against his ribs, like a second heart.

"Alright," Elias said to the empty air. "Alright. I hear you."

He turned toward the village.

The walk back was a blur. The cold wind whipped at his face, but he didn’t feel it. The ring’s warmth spread through his limbs, making his steps light, his mind sharp. He thought of the plow, left abandoned in the furrow. He thought of the potatoes, rotting in the cellar. He thought of Mordecai’s copper robes, the way the High Priest looked at him with those tired, knowing eyes.

Let him look. Let him see.

Elias walked faster. The ridge of the Hill of Bones loomed to the north, a jagged scar against the gray. He avoided it, as everyone did. The ground there hummed, made livestock blind. But today, the hum sounded like music. It sounded like a promise.

He reached the edge of the Valley of Weeping Stone. The mist thinned, revealing the cluster of mud-brick huts of Ostrava. Smoke rose from the chimneys, thin and gray. The village was quiet. Too quiet.

Elias stepped onto the main path. The iron nails in his boots began to sing.

He felt it before he heard it. A high-pitched whine, rising in pitch. The nails in his soles grew hot. He bit down on his lip, tasting copper. The heat bit into his feet, but he didn’t stop. He couldn’t stop. The ring pulled at him, a gravitational tug toward the center of the square.

He kept walking.

The doors opened. One by one. Faces appeared in the doorways. Gaunt. Hollow. Eyes wide.

Elias walked into the square.

The villagers stood back, forming a wide circle. They didn’t speak. They didn’t cheer. They watched. Their breath plumed in the air, white clouds in the gray light.

Mordecai was there.

The High Priest stood near the well, his copper-robe wires humming so loudly that the sound grated against Elias’s teeth. Mordecai’s face was pale, his eyes wide and dark. He held a staff of twisted rebar, his knuckles white.

"Boy," Mordecai said. His voice was a rasp, like sandpaper on stone. "Put it down."

Elias stopped. He looked at the old man. He felt the ring pulse against his chest, urging him forward. *Hold on,* the voice whispered. *You are safe.*

"It’s not a toy, Mordecai," Elias said. His voice was steady. Strong. "It’s the seed."

"It’s a wound," Mordecai snapped. He took a step forward, the wires of his robe sparking with static. "It’s a hole in the sky. You don’t plant holes. You fill them."

Kael emerged from the shadow of the smithy.

He was lean, wired tight with nervous energy. His eyes were red-rimmed, his skin the color of old parchment. He smiled, but it didn’t reach his eyes. It was a baring of teeth.

"Seed?" Kael laughed. It was a wet, guttural sound. "You call that a seed, Elias? I’ve seen seeds. They don’t glow. They don’t hum."

He stepped into the circle. The villagers parted for him. They always did.

"Give it here," Kael said. He extended a hand. His fingers were long, crooked, stained with grease and blood. "Let me see if it bites."

Elias took a step back. The ring grew warmer. *Hold on.*

"No," Elias said.

Kael’s smile widened. "You’re shaking, farmer. You’re shaking like a leaf. Hand it over. Before you drop it. Before it breaks."

Mordecai raised his staff. "Kael, stand down."

"Make me, Old Rust," Kael spat. He lunged.

He moved fast, faster than a man his size should. He closed the distance in two strides. Elias tried to pull away, but the ring’s field reacted. It didn’t push Kael back. It bent the air around them.

Kael’s hand, reaching for Elias’s tunic, stopped inches from the fabric. The air around his fingers shimmered, then distorted. The rusted pipe at his hip twisted, the metal flowing like soft wax.

Kael laughed. He didn’t look at the pipe. He looked at Elias.

"Pretty," Kael said.

He tackled Elias.

They hit the ground hard. The impact knocked the wind out of Elias. The bundle slipped from his grip.

Time slowed.

Elias watched the apron fly through the air. The silver ring tumbled out, spinning faster, faster, until it was a blur of light. It didn’t hit the ground.

It hovered.

Three feet in the air.

The hum became a scream.

The air around the ring crystallized. Frost raced across the cobblestones, shooting toward Elias and Kael like veins of ice. It hit Kael’s legs. The scavenger screamed, but the sound was sucked out of his throat, replaced by the crackle of freezing water.

Elias reached for the ring.

*Hold on,* the voice commanded. *You are safe.*

He grabbed it.

The cold vanished. The heat returned, violent and absolute. The frost on his legs melted instantly, turning to steam. The ring spun in his palm, syncing with his heartbeat. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

He stood up.

Kael was on his knees, shivering, his skin turning blue. He looked up, his eyes wide with terror.

"What is it?" Kael wheezed. "What is it?"

Elias looked down at the ring. He looked at Mordecai, who was backing away, his staff dropped in the dirt. He looked at the villagers, who were pressing against the walls, their faces masks of awe and horror.

Elias smiled. It was a small, tight thing.

"It’s the answer," he said.

He wrapped his fingers tighter around the silver. The heat burned his skin, but he didn’t let go. He felt the ring drinking the warmth from the air, from the ground, from the people around him. He felt stronger. He felt heavier.

He looked up at the sky. The clouds were swirling, forming a perfect, unnatural vortex above the valley. The light within them was too bright, too white.

Elias didn’t look away.

He held the ring high.

"Look," he said to the sky. "I’m here."
