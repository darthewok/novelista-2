### Structural Summary
Mika, a hedgehog paralyzed by the memory of getting lost in the tall grass, must leave the safety of her oak-root door when the Moonberry vanishes, silencing the fireflies’ song. The story follows a tight try-fail arc: first, she attempts to find the berry using the fireflies’ lights, which fail; second, she tries to follow the mouse Bramble’s pragmatic map, which fails; finally, she succeeds by trusting her own senses—her nose and her heart—rather than external guidance. The narrative moves from the claustrophobic comfort of the oak to the terrifying vastness of the meadow, culminating in a quiet moment of courage where Mika retrieves the berry not by seeing the path, but by *feeling* it.

### Ch 1: The Silence of the Silver Reed
*   **POV:** Mika (Third-person limited).
*   **Beats:**
    *   **Opening Image:** Mika sits at her round oak door, counting breaths (one, two, three) to steady her quills. The meadow is dark; the fireflies are mute. The absence of light is heavy, like wet wool.
    *   **Inciting Incident:** Bramble the mouse arrives, chewing mint furiously. He announces the Moonberry is gone. Without it, the fireflies will forget their songs forever. The silence is already pressing against the oak’s roots.
    *   **Debate/Refusal:** Mika wants to help but fears the "Wide Open." She recites her lie: "I am too small for the wide sky." She points out the darkness. Bramble offers a map drawn on birch bark, insisting she can do it if she follows the "straight line."
    *   **Break into Two:** A single, weak spark flickers from the reeds—a dying firefly. It’s not just dark; it’s forgetting. Mika realizes her fear of being lost is worse than the fear of the dark. She steps over the threshold.
    *   **Fun & Games / First Attempt:** Mika tries to use Bramble’s map. She walks straight, but the grass grows taller, swallowing her view. She loses the line. Panic rises in her throat. She feels the ground shift. She retreats to a root, trembling.
    *   **Midpoint / Second Attempt:** Old Fen the firefly hums a low, sad note. He reminds her of the "long-ago" loss. He suggests following the scent of silver. Mika tries to follow the smell, but the wind shifts, leading her in circles. She is dizzy. The silence is growing louder.
    *   **All Is Lost:** Mika’s quills bristle in terror. The meadow feels like a mouth. She sits down, ready to give up. She believes she is indeed too small, too broken, too lost. The firefly’s light dims to a grey ember.
    *   **Dark Night / Break into Three:** Mika closes her eyes. She stops looking for the map. She stops listening to the silence. She focuses on her nose. She remembers the smell of the berry from the one time she got close as a kit—sweet, cold, like rain on stone. She trusts her body, not her eyes. She moves forward by scent and sound of her own paws.
    *   **Finale:** She finds the reed. The berry is stuck in a clump of thorny vines. She cannot see it clearly, but she can feel its cold pulse against her nose. She uses her small claws to carefully untangle the vines, one by one. Her paws are steady. She pulls the berry free.
    *   **Final Image:** Mika carries the berry back. She doesn’t run. She walks slowly. As she approaches the oak, the berry glows, and the fireflies begin to hum again. Mika doesn’t look up at the sky. She looks at her paws. They are just right.
*   **Emotional Arc:** Fear -> Confusion -> Despair -> Clarity -> Quiet Pride.
*   **Plants/Payoffs:**
    *   *Plant:* Mika counts breaths at the start. *Payoff:* She uses breath to steady her paws at the end.
    *   *Plant:* Bramble mentions the berry smells like "rain on stone." *Payoff:* Mika uses this scent to navigate in the dark.
    *   *Plant:* Mika’s quills bristle when she’s scared. *Payoff:* Her quills settle when she focuses on her nose.
*   **Out-the-Door Hook:** The firefly’s light is a dying ember. If Mika doesn’t move now, the song is gone forever.
