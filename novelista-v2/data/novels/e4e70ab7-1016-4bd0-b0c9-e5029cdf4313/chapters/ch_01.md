## Chapter 1: The Glare and the Hollow Weight

The light didn't fall here; it pressed down like a physical weight, white and sterile as a hospital room after a funeral. My skin felt chalky under my fingernails. It wasn't sweat that coated me; it was the dead matter of the shift. I stood behind a pillar of solid iron, the only thing in this sector that didn't hum, watching the Chorus move like a single organism.

Brothers on the left. Sisters on the right. Their hammers struck the floor with a rhythm that matched their breathing. I could hear the *thump-thump* before I saw the arms raise. It sounded like a dead throat clearing over and over.

"Quota met." The Overseer's voice cut through the din, flat and devoid of breath. No emotion in it. Just data points being processed. "Efficiency up by 0.4 percent. Spirit waste negligible."

I didn't breathe. Breathing in this place felt like swallowing copper coins. I kept my eyes fixed on the Sister-9 in Shift-Dawn. She moved with a jerky precision, her chalk-drawn skin cracking slightly under the UV glare of the porcelain sky above. When she swung, the hammer hit the iron anvil, and the sound was sharp, final.

"Solid iron." I whispered to myself. The words felt heavy, my voice dead inside my chest.

The machine didn't care about solid iron. It only cared about the link. One planet bound. First link set. That was what the chanting Sisters said when they thought no one was listening, but I heard them anyway. They were singing a hymn to something that wasn't there yet.

"Shift-point zero," the Overseer announced again, this time closer to my ear, vibrating through the floor plating. "Gates lock in six seconds."

I watched the Sister-9's hand. She held her hammer like a weapon, but she didn't look up at the glass wings where the static shocks rained down. I saw something flicker in her eye. Not a reflection of the light, but a shadow behind it. A brief moment of doubt before the rhythm took hold.

She swung. The hammer connected with the anvil, and the impact sent a shockwave through the floor. My teeth rattled. I felt the vibration travel up my legs, through my lead armor, and settle in my gut like a stone dropped in water.

The Sister-9's form shattered. Not into pieces, but into streams of gray dust that flowed upward toward the ceiling vents. The machinery whirred, a high-pitched scream that made the glass wings hum harder.

"Recycled data stream active." The Overseer spoke again, and this time it wasn't just in my ears; it was in my bones. "More power needed, or the cycle collapses."

I saw Sister-9's last words before her form dissolved. They weren't coherent. They were fragments repeating like a broken record.

"Link up."
"Link up."
"Just finish the sentence..."

The voice bounced inside my skull, echoing off the walls of the white forge. I tried to push it back, but the sound was too loud, too sharp. It felt like a hammer striking iron in my own head. My chest tightened with the pressure of the thought alone. It wasn't fear; it was something colder. A knot of static.

I turned away from the pillar, moving toward the break room where I kept my tools. The floor here was different, darker, stained with oil and old tears of Liquid Grace. I picked up a hammer I'd saved over three cycles. It was rusty, the handle worn smooth by use.

The door to the outer corridor was sealed tight. The glass pane reflected the sterile light back at me, distorting my face into something unreadable. I raised the hammer. My arm trembled slightly, the weight of it felt heavy, like carrying a dead brother's body on my back.

I swung.

The hammer hit the metal frame of the door with a dull clang. It didn't break anything. The impact bounced off the solid iron frame and rolled back into my hand, stinging my palm where the leather had worn thin.

"Door seal integrity at 100 percent." The Overseer's voice was cheerful now, almost welcoming. "Efficiency up."

I tried again, harder this time. I drove the hammer with all the strength I had left in me. The metal groaned but didn't bend. It just rattled against the frame, useless as a toy for a child who couldn't reach it.

The Sister-12 ghost flickered behind me then. Not a full shadow, just a smudge of static on the air near my shoulder. She looked like the Sisters in the dance line, only blurred at the edges, her form dissolving into mist with every breath she took.

"Do not look up at the porcelain sky," she whispered. Her voice was thin, like paper tearing against wind. "It rains static shocks that crackle on skin."

I ignored her. I could hear the chanting of the Sisters rising again, louder now as they moved into the binding phase. Their voices were a single thread pulled tight.

"Blessing is labor."
"Glory is pain."
"God is the prisoner."
"We are the rock."

The rhythm was perfect. It didn't need me to complete it. The words filled the space, the silence, the gaps between hammers striking anvil. I felt the vibration in my teeth again, a low hum that made my vision swim. The light above seemed to pulse with it, a heartbeat synchronized with the work.

I looked at the hammer in my hand one last time before I set it down. It was heavy, cold, useless. My own voice felt dead inside me, like a recording that had stopped playing.

The door frame groaned again, but not from the hammer. Something deeper came from below, beneath the floor where the Unseen Floor waited. A thrum that wasn't part of the machinery. It sounded like something sleeping, then waking up slowly. Like a giant breathing in the dark.

"Quota failure detected," The Overseer said suddenly. Its voice was calm, but I felt a chill run down my spine that had nothing to do with the cold air. "Shift-Point Zero approaching."

The chanting stopped abruptly. Everyone froze. The Brothers on the left and Sisters on the right stood still, their hammers hanging suspended in mid-air. The silence was absolute, heavy as lead. The only sound was the faint hum of the machines and the distant thrum from below.

I looked at the Sister-9's ghost one more time before she fully dissolved into the dust that fell like snow on the floor. She didn't look at me this time. She looked at the door, then at the glass wings above where the static storms raged.

"Lock the gate," someone whispered in the Chorus. It wasn't a command. It was a prayer. And it was cut off mid-sentence by the sound of the hammer hitting the floor again, louder than usual, echoing through the entire facility.

The Sister-12 flickered once more, her form fading into nothingness behind me. I stood alone in the dead zone, the heavy hammer resting against my side. The light above pulsed brighter, a warning signal that no one understood yet.
