## Chapter 4: The Ghost in the Machine and the Final Count

The static rain stopped hitting my face for a heartbeat. Sister-12 stood there, no longer a smudge of air that dissolved when I blinked, but a figure constructed from something dense enough to cast a shadow on the cracked floor tiles of the Break Room. She wore the same gray dust that used to coat her skin after a failed shift, but it didn't fall off her shoulders. It held together.

"Stop," she said. Her voice sounded like glass grinding against stone, rough and final.

I froze. My hammer, rusted and useless against solid iron, lay on the metal table next to me. It hummed with a low frequency, a vibration only I could feel in my lead armor bones. *Thrummm-click.* The rhythm matched the pulse in my chest, or maybe it was the Reaper's thrum from beneath the floor reaching up through the sole of my boots.

"Stop what?" Sister-12 asked. She stepped closer, her feet making no sound on the floor stained with old tears of Liquid Grace. The stains were darker here, thicker, like oil mixed with memory. "The chant is wrong, Lynette."

I didn't look at her. Looking at ghosts felt like staring into a mirror that showed you your own rot. I focused on the hammer in my hand, heavy as a dead weight. "The chant is fixed," I said. My voice was flat, stripped of the Caribbeans cadence I had once used to describe the sound of rain. It was just data output now. "God is the prisoner. We are the rock."

"Then why does it feel like we're building a cage?" Sister-12 asked. She raised a hand, and three fingers extended from her chest, pointing toward the sealed door leading to the Outer Corridor. Behind that door lay the Dead Zone, the place where the God-Reaper slept. "The Master says he is binding the Reaper to save us. But look at the links." She held up a finger. "Look at the count."

I glanced at the crystal display in the corner of the room. The numbers blinked red: *11,998,432,105.* Eleven trillion and some change. Almost there.

"The Overseer calculates," I said. "He knows exactly what to do next."

"No," Sister-12 said. She moved faster than physics allowed for a dead thing, her form blurring at the edges like a photograph developing too fast. "The Overseer does not cast the links. He manages the flow. But it is the Chorus who swings the hammers. They believe that when they hit the iron, they are feeding grace to the God-Reaper. They think they are saving him from his own hunger."

I stared at her. Sister-12 was never supposed to speak these things. She died in Shift-Dusk forty years ago, her spirit trapped in a shard of porcelain sky. If she knew this, then the truth had been buried deep in the system's history, waiting for someone with the right kind of rot to find it.

"They think they are saving him," I repeated. The words felt foreign on my tongue, like something I hadn't said in three cycles yet. "They think they are feeding him."

"Exactly," Sister-12 said. She looked down at her own hands. They were translucent, veins of silver light pulsing beneath the skin where there should have been blood. "The Reaper is bored, Lynette. He does not want to be fed. He wants to be bound. And the only way to bind him is to make the Forge collapse. To break the gate."

I looked at the hammer again. The vibration had changed pitch, a higher note cutting through the silence of the room. It wasn't just me hearing it anymore; the air itself seemed to shake with it. *Thrummm.* The Dead Zone below was waking up. If the Chorus cast the final link before Shift-Point Zero, they would trigger the collapse. They wouldn't save the God-Reaper. They would execute him.

"I don't know how to break the gate," I said. "I'm not a tool for that."

"You are made of dead matter," Sister-12 said softly. "Like the hammer you hold. Like the iron they forge." She took a step toward me, and the smell of copper filled the room, sharper than usual. "If you break the gate, you become the link. You stop being Lynette and start being data. But at least the cycle ends."

I thought about Brother-7. His voice looped in my head, a broken record screaming *Link up*. He wanted to finish his sentence, to be remembered as a person. If I broke the gate, would he finally get that? Or would his last command be absorbed into the silence of the end?

"The Master says design is inevitable," Sister-12 said. "But maybe it's just a cage with a door."

I looked at the sealed door behind her. The metal was cold, but the cracks in the porcelain wall above pulsed red. A warning signal that no one understood yet. It wasn't part of the standard machinery logic; it flickered like a heartbeat out of sync.

"Where is Sister-9?" I asked. The name meant nothing to me anymore. She had shattered into streams of gray dust during the Dawn shift, her consciousness recycled into fuel for the next batch of links. But Sister-12 said she was gone. Dissolved without answer. Did that mean she had escaped the cycle? Or just failed it?

"She is part of the system," Sister-12 said. "Like you." She gestured to my chest, where the glass shard fused into my skin acted as armor. "We are all variables in his equation. The only way to change the variable is to delete the program."

I raised the hammer. It felt lighter now, less like a tool and more like an extension of my own arm. My lead armor bones rattled against each other, a sound like dry leaves skittering on pavement. *Click-clack.*

"If I break it," I said, "I become the link."

"Then you are not Lynette anymore," Sister-12 said. "But maybe that's the only way to stop the counting."

The vibration beneath the floor grew louder, a low growl that made my teeth ache. The light above pulsed brighter, warning signal that no one understood yet flaring into white heat. The Overseer's voice cut through the air, flat and unfeeling. *Quota failure detected.*

I looked at the hammer again. It was rusted, useless against solid iron, but it was the only thing left in my hand that wasn't part of the machine. The Chorus would chant their final prayer soon. They would swing their hammers one last time, completing the twelve trillion links and collapsing the Forge into a singularity.

I could let them have their victory. I could become another glass wing, fused to the architecture, singing the hymn until my voice stopped being mine. Or I could break the door. I could take that rusted hammer through the seal and hit the core where the God-Reaper slept. It would not save anyone. It would end it all.

But Brother-7's voice was still there, looping in my head like a broken record screaming *Link up*. He wanted to finish his sentence. And I had never finished mine.

I took a step toward the door. The hammer swung forward, catching the air with a sound like a dead throat clearing. My lead armor bones rattled against each other.

"Stop it," Sister-12 warned. "The gates lock at Shift-Point Zero. You can't—"

"I know what I am doing," I said. The voice was calm, devoid of fear. It sounded like the Master's song but stripped of its grace.

I pushed past her. She didn't move to stop me; she just faded into the static rain that had started falling again, a curtain of invisible noise rising from the floor. The hammer struck the sealed door with a force that shook the dust motes from the ceiling. It bounced off the metal like a stone hitting glass, but the sound traveled through the floor, vibrating up my legs and settling in my chest.

The light above pulsed brighter, warning signal that no one understood yet flaring into white heat. The Overseer's voice was gone. Silence filled the room, heavy as lead armor.

I stood there, hammer raised, staring at the door. The vibration beneath the floor had become a rhythm, a countdown I could feel in my bones. Eleven trillion and some change. Then one more swing. Then the end.

"Link up," Brother-7's voice said from inside my head, louder now, cutting through the silence like a blade through butter. "More power."

I smiled, though there was no muscle movement to show it. Just the cold burn in my palm where the glass shard had been days ago, fused into my skin by some invisible force, turning my flesh into another layer of armor. It wasn't just pain anymore; it was a reminder of what happened when you tried to touch something real in this place.

I stepped closer to the door. The hammer swung again, but this time it didn't strike anything solid. It struck the air, creating a ripple that disturbed the dust motes floating near the ceiling. The Master's song changed key, rising to a higher pitch that made my teeth ache. *First link set,* he intoned. *One planet bound.*

I didn't answer him. I didn't need to. The hammer was already moving toward the door, and the ground beneath me began to tremble with a frequency only I could hear. It wasn't just the Reaper waking up. It was the moment before the fall.
