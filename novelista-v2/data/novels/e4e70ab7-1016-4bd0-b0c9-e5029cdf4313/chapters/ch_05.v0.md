## Chapter 5: The Calcified Will and the Unseen Floor

The air inside the White Forge tasted like cold prayer, sharp enough to cut your tongue when you swallowed it. I woke up gasping, my lungs full of smoke that felt lighter than a funeral shroud, as if I was carrying the weight of an unburied spirit. The light here wasn't sun at all, but a sterile glare from porcelain skylights that hummed with a low frequency, vibrating in my teeth.

"Shift-point zero," a voice called out, not from a speaker but from the air itself, crisp and clear against the din of the machines. "Gate locks in six seconds. Initiate binding protocol." The word *protocol* hung heavy in the humid heat, mocking the idea of salvation. A man to my left stumbled over his station, his eyes rolling back as the machinery hummed him into a cylinder of recycled data. He was gone before I could scream, his spirit reduced to nothing more than fuel for the next batch.

I did not look at the man who turned into dust. I looked at the door. The seal had held long enough for the rhythm to build. Eleven trillion and some change. Then one more swing. Then the end.

My lead armor bones rattled against each other as I moved through the corridor. Chalk-drawn skin made my movement sound like dragging a skeleton on pavement, but the floor absorbed the impact. It was the only thing that felt solid in this place. Solid iron didn't care if you broke it; Liquid Grace cared how much of you you left behind.

I reached the Outer Corridor. The air here was thicker, tasting of copper and old rain, the residue of Spirit Meals chewed by the Chorus in the Dawn shift. Sister-9's ghost hung over the threshold like a smudge of static on the air, blurred at the edges, dissolving with every breath she took. She repeated "Link up" again, but her voice was thinner now, stripped of context until it sounded less like a plea and more like a test.

I pushed past her. She didn't move to stop me; she just faded into the static rain that had started falling again, a curtain of invisible noise rising from the floor. The hammer struck the sealed door with a force that shook the dust motes from the ceiling. It bounced off the metal like a stone hitting glass, but the sound traveled through the floor, vibrating up my legs and settling in my chest.

The vibration beneath the floor had become a rhythm, a countdown I could feel in my bones. Eleven trillion and some change. Then one more swing. Then the end.

"Link up," Brother-7's voice said from inside my head, louder now, cutting through the silence like a blade through butter. "More power."

I smiled, though there was no muscle movement to show it. Just the cold burn in my palm where the glass shard had been days ago, fused into my skin by some invisible force, turning my flesh into another layer of armor. It wasn't just pain anymore; it was a reminder of what happened when you tried to touch something real in this place.

The hammer swung again, but this time it didn't strike anything solid. It struck the air, creating a ripple that disturbed the dust motes floating near the ceiling. The Master's song changed key, rising to a higher pitch that made my teeth ache. *First link set,* he intoned. *One planet bound.*

I didn't answer him. I didn't need to. The hammer was already moving toward the door, and the ground beneath me began to tremble with a frequency only I could hear. It wasn't just the Reaper waking up. It was the moment before the fall.

The light above pulsed brighter, warning signal that no one understood yet flaring into white heat. The Overseer's voice was gone. Silence filled the room, heavy as lead armor.
