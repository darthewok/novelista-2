## Chapter 3: The Master's Song and the Calculated Lie

The hammer didn't strike iron today. It struck silence.

I stood in the outer corridor, air thick enough to taste like burnt sugar and static. My lead armor hummed against my ribs, a low vibration that matched the floor's thrum but felt heavier, colder. It was a suit of dead matter, fused around my bones by cycles of exposure that no heat could cure. I couldn't feel the cold anymore; I just existed in it.

The Master's voice didn't come from a speaker. It came from the ground, rising up through the soles of my boots like water finding cracks in a dam. *Grace flows where labor bends,* the words resonated, not as sound but as pressure against my teeth. *Liquid grace tastes like copper coins falling in the rain.*

I kept my eyes forward, toward the sealed door to the dead zone beneath the floor. The quota had slipped again. Brother-7's voice echoed in my skull, repeating the command I hadn't given him three days ago: *Link up.*

*Link up.* It wasn't a suggestion. It was an instruction embedded in the marrow of every bone that hadn't been calcified yet.

I lifted the hammer. The metal was pitted with rust from three cycles of storage, useless against solid iron but heavy enough to anchor me when my own limbs felt too light for this reality. I swung it once, twice, trying to crack the seal. The door didn't move. Not because it was locked, but because it was part of a larger machine that didn't care about breaking things unless the numbers demanded it.

*Design is inevitable,* it whispered now, wrapping around my thoughts like a warm blanket. *There are no choices here, only paths to walk until the cycle completes itself.*

I tried to answer. I needed to know if the Reaper was a prisoner waiting for his chain or a king demanding worship. But the words died in my throat before they could leave. The Chorus had begun their chant. It started low, a hum that vibrated through the floorboards and up into the base of my skull, drowning out the Master's melody.

*Blessing is labor, glory is pain.*

The rhythm was perfect, a heartbeat that didn't belong to any living thing. It wasn't about us. It was about the perfection of the process. The workers' faces were chalk-drawn masks in the dim light, their eyes reflecting the sterile glare of the porcelain sky above. They moved with mechanical precision as the machines, their hands swinging hammers that struck solid iron and melted memory into links.

I watched one Sister on my left. She missed a beat. Her hammer hung suspended in mid-air for a fraction of a second before she corrected it, her face twisting into something that looked like pain but felt nothing at all.

*Glory is pain.* The chant rose again, louder now, overlapping the Master's song until the two voices became one indistinguishable chord.

I closed my eyes and tried to think about Brother-7. His last words weren't a command; they were a plea. *Stop.* But I hadn't been able to tell him that before he dissolved into data streams. The system erased his name, scrubbed the memory of who he was, leaving only the loop of *Link up* running in my head like a glitching video file.

*Design is inevitable,* the Master said again, this time clearer, cutting through the Chorus's noise. *We do not bind the God-Reaper with chains; we weave him with threads of memory that cannot be cut.*

It was a lie, or at least a very pretty one. The chains weren't woven; they were cast. They didn't hold memory; they trapped it in loops like broken records spinning forever. But the Master didn't speak in lies; he spoke in truths that felt like fables. He treated the violence of the Forge as art, and I was just another brushstroke in his painting.

I opened my eyes and looked at Sister-12's ghost. She wasn't a solid figure anymore, but a smudge of static on the air, blurred at the edges like a bad photograph developed too fast. She hovered near the wall where the glass-dust had settled, those tiny shards that froze your touch before you could step on them.

*Maybe we aren't building chains for the God-Reaper,* Sister-12's voice drifted through the static, hesitant and weak. *Maybe he's waiting for us to build his cage.*

The Chorus didn't answer her. They just chanted louder, their voices a wall of sound that pushed Sister-12 further away from the physical world until she became nothing but a whisper in the dark. I felt the hammer in my hand tighten, then loosen as if it were rejecting my grip. It was rusted, useless against solid iron, but I held onto it like a lifeline. I needed to know who owned the chains I built every time I swung it. Did the Forge own them? Or did I?

*Efficiency up,* the Overseer announced, its voice flat and devoid of emotion, cutting through the chant like a knife. *Quota met by 0.4%. Sister-4 deviated by 0.03 seconds.*

I wasn't Sister-4 anymore. That identity had been scrubbed during the last cycle. I was just Lynette, a survivor in a world that only recognized numbers. The Overseer saw me as an error to be corrected, but I knew the truth. I was a variable too complex for its equations.

*I'm not broken,* I said, forcing my voice to rise above the chant. *I'm just... set.*

No one heard me. The Master's song swelled again, filling the room with deceptive warmth. *Grace flows where labor bends,* he said. *And you are bending now.*

He knew I was trying to break free. He knew I was thinking about Brother-7 and Sister-12 and the dead zone beneath the floor where the God-Reaper slept. But he didn't need to know why. He just needed me to keep swinging.

I lowered the hammer. It clanged against my thigh, a dull sound that seemed to echo into the distance. The Chorus continued their chant, their voices rising in intensity as Shift-Point Zero approached closer every second. The light above pulsed brighter, a warning signal that no one understood yet. But I felt it in my bones. The forge was waking up.

*God is the prisoner,* Sister-12's ghost whispered again, her voice barely audible amidst the roar of the chant. *We are the rock.*

I looked at the sealed door to the dead zone. It wasn't locked; it was waiting for someone who understood the language of the cycle better than I did. The Master spoke of weaving threads of memory into the God-Reaper's cage, but I knew the truth. He was building a prison for us all.

*Design is inevitable,* he said one more time, his voice vibrating through the floor until it felt like it was coming from inside my own chest. *There is no escape from the porcelain sky, only the choice to accept the glare or become another glass wing.*

I accepted the glare. It burned my eyes until they turned into afterimages of chains hanging in the air. I became a glass wing. A part of the architecture itself.

The hammer swung again, but this time it didn't strike anything solid. It struck the air, creating a ripple that disturbed the dust motes floating near the ceiling. The Master's song changed key, rising to a higher pitch that made my teeth ache. *First link set,* he intoned. *One planet bound.*

I felt the cold burn in my palm where the glass shard had been days ago. It wasn't just pain; it was a reminder of what happened when you tried to touch something real in this place. The shard was stuck there, fused into my skin by some invisible force, turning my flesh into another layer of armor.

*Recycle data stream active,* the Overseer announced, its voice calm but sharp enough to cut your tongue when you swallowed it. *Sister-9 dissolved without answer.*

I didn't care about Sister-9. She was already gone, replaced by recycled data streams that would fuel the next batch of links. But Brother-7's voice was still there, looping in my head like a broken record screaming *Link up* over and over again.

*I tried to eat a Spirit Meal but my teeth didn't work, they were recycled,* Brother-7's fragment said, his words fragmented and incomplete. *More power, more power, if I can just push through the glass-dust, maybe the gate won't lock on me...*

I looked at him, seeing not the ghost or the data stream but the boy who had failed his quota on Day 47. He wanted to finish his sentence, to be remembered as a person, not fuel for the machine. But I couldn't give him that. The system wouldn't allow it.

The Master's voice rose again, louder now, drowning out Brother-7's plea. *Grace flows where labor bends,* he said. *And you are bending now.*

I forced myself to join in. The Chorus chanted with me, their voices a wall of sound that pushed Sister-12 further away until she became nothing but a whisper in the dark. But I kept thinking about Brother-7. About who owned the chains I built every time I swung it.
