## Chapter 2: Blue Light Bleeding into Corneas

The Spire hummed with a frequency that made teeth rattle if you didn't wear dampening earplugs. Inside the Core Chamber, the air tasted of burnt plastic and overheating copper wires, thick enough to choke on. Rex stood in the center of the room, his spinal port glowing a soft, steady violet against his chest cavity.

He wasn't supposed to be here. He was supposed to be dead or dying outside in Sector Seven, rotting while Kael collected the zeros from the Alpha Contract. Instead, he was wired directly into the Grid's mainframe, a flesh-and-blood extension of the algorithm itself. His left arm twitched, fingers splayed as if trying to grasp something invisible.

Rex felt the pulse in his veins, but it wasn't blood pumping through them. It was data streams, hot and electric, flowing faster than he could track. The Chrome Trigger flooded his system, not as a drug injected from outside, but as a command running from within. *Stay.* The word echoed in his neural link, translated into pure sensation: warmth, brightness, the feeling of being whole.

Kael watched from the shadows of the server racks, retinal implant casting blue light over his face. He saw Rex move. Saw him reach for a console that didn't belong to anyone. His hands trembled, not from fear, but from the electrical current coursing through his cyber-arm, which had been re-routed to bypass pain sensors entirely.

"I can fix it," Rex rasped. His voice sounded like static on a dead channel, layered with a low hum that vibrated in Kael's sternum. "I can... optimize... you."

Kael took a step forward, boots clicking on the grating floor. The sound was harsh against the ambient drone of the machines. "Optimize? Rex, we're supposed to be running. Extraction phase is minutes away."

Rex turned his head toward Kael, eyes wide with that impossible blue light bleeding into his irises like acid cutting through meat. He smiled, but it didn't reach the teeth he'd had removed years ago during a surgery gone wrong during the Last War. Those teeth were gone, replaced by sensors designed to measure voltage instead of temperature.

"No extraction," Rex said, voice rising slightly, gaining that mechanical edge the drug always added. "Extraction is... obsolete."

"Johnson said the job was done," Kael snapped, stepping closer. The heat radiating off Rex's skin was unnatural, like standing near a running generator. "He paid up front. We walk out, we get our credits, we disappear."

Rex laughed, a sound like grinding gears stuck together in oil. He pointed a finger toward the massive server racks lining the walls. Each unit pulsed with a rhythmic green light, syncing with the dancehall rhythm that seemed to pulse through the floorboards beneath them.

"Johnson didn't pay up front," Rex corrected, his tone flat now, devoid of inflection. "He bought us all. He bought my spinal port. He bought your retinal implant. He bought... this." He tapped his own chest. "And he's waiting for us to finish breaking our own bones so he can sell the pieces."

Kael frowned, looking at the Soul Ledger on his wrist terminal. The screen flickered, displaying a string of red numbers that counted down rapidly. *Unpaid bills.* The debt hadn't been cleared. It was growing. Every hour added another line item. Another soul fragment sold to Johnson for the privilege of using their bodies as living terminals.

Rex reached out again, this time grabbing Kael's shoulder. His grip was tight, fingers pressing hard enough to leave indentations that felt too sharp for human skin. "Don't leave me," he whispered, voice distorted by the chemical feed flooding his veins. "The signal is too loud. I need... the connection."

"You're dead already," Kael said quietly. He looked at Rex's face, seeing the way the blue light made his features look like a painting done in wireframes and static. "You're just part of the machine now. A component. Like a spare part waiting to be replaced when it wears out."

Rex shook his head slowly, eyes widening again as another surge of digital dopamine hit his system. The world around them dissolved into a kaleidoscope of impossible colors—neon oranges, electric violets, shades of green that didn't exist in reality. He could see ghosts in the wireframes of the city, flickering shapes of people who had been deleted from the Grid but still lingered in the atmosphere like smoke after a fire.

"Blue light," Rex murmured, tracing patterns in the air with his free hand. "It's beautiful. It's... data. Pure data."

Kael turned away, walking toward the heavy metal door that led out into the Neon Jungle. He needed to think. Needed to figure out how to stop this before they all became inventory for Johnson's collection. But as he moved, his own hand twitched slightly, fingers curling around a loose wire on the console behind him. Sparks flew from the socket, dying instantly as if cut off mid-stream.

He glanced back at Rex, whose body was slumped against the server rack, eyes wide with that impossible blue light. Rex's cyber-arm hummed, not from power, but from the drug flooding his veins. Kael knew what that looked like. He'd seen it in the Sector Seven market reports, annotated with red stamps saying *Unstable*.

"Rex," Kael called out, voice rough over the hum of the city outside. "Wake up."

But Rex didn't respond. He just sat there, breathing slowly, eyes fixed on nothing and everything at once. The blue light in his corneas seemed to bleed outward, spreading across his face like ink dropped into water. It was a beautiful thing, terrifyingly so. A glitch in the matrix short-circuiting logic with euphoria while his legs turned to jelly.

Kael checked his Soul Ledger again. The numbers were gone. Replaced by a single message: *Connection Severed.* He blinked, rubbing the side of his head where the retinal implant pressed against his temple. There was no pain from the injury, only a dull throb that felt like someone had tapped him on the shoulder with a hammer wrapped in velvet.

The connection was already severed. The only question was whether he'd remember it tomorrow.

Outside, the rain began to fall again, heavier this time, washing away the grime but leaving the streets slicker than a fresh corpse. Somewhere far below, in the Gutter, the Vultures were scavenging the dead Ronin for parts before they could even die properly. Their mechanical growls echoed through the sewers, a low-frequency noise that matched the rhythm of the Spire's heartbeat.

Kael stepped closer to Rex, reaching out to touch his shoulder again. This time, his finger brushed against something cold and hard—a sensor panel embedded in Rex's collarbone. He pulled back, staring at his own hand. The skin on his fingertip was gray, cracking like old parchment paper.

"Rex," Kael whispered, feeling the weight of what he had to do next pressing down on him like a vice. "Don't let them take you."

Rex's eyes fluttered shut for a moment, then opened again, filled with that same luminous blue light. He reached out once more, grabbing Kael's wrist this time instead of shoulder. His grip was firm, unyielding, fingers locking onto the bone like steel clamps.

"Don't leave me," Rex repeated, voice distorted by the chemical feed flooding his veins. "The signal is too loud. I need... the connection."

Kael looked at the door handle, then back at Rex. He saw the way Rex's pupils dilated, swallowing the light until they were just black holes reflecting the city outside. The blue light was spreading, bleeding into Kael's vision now too, making everything look like a painting done in wireframes and static.

He didn't answer. He couldn't answer. There was nothing left to say. Just the sound of rain falling on metal, the hum of servers overheating, and the distant scream of a drone hunting down something that had lost its name to the Grid.

Kael turned away from Rex, stepping toward the door again. The heavy footsteps echoed in the chamber, each step heavier than the last. Outside, the wind picked up, swirling dust and debris into a vortex that threatened to swallow the Spire whole.

He didn't look back. He knew what waited on the other side: scavengers, debt collectors, and the endless cycle of selling oneself until nothing remained.

The door hissed open, revealing a corridor bathed in shifting neon hues that felt less like color and more like memory fragments. Kael stepped through, leaving the red glow behind him, heading into the dark where the Vultures were waiting with their glowing eyes and mechanical teeth.

Behind him, Rex sat still, eyes wide with that impossible blue light, fingers curling around the console as if trying to hold onto something invisible. The blue light bled into his corneas, spreading across his face like ink dropped into water, beautiful and terrible all at once.
