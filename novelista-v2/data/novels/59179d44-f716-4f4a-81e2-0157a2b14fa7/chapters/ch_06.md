## Chapter 6: Leave the Meat for the Rats

The rain in the Gutter didn't wash anything. It just pooled, turning the toxic black water into a slick mirror that reflected neon signs flickering erratically, as if the city were choking on its own exhaust. Kael moved through the labyrinth of sewage tunnels, boots clicking on asphalt slicker than a fresh corpse. He wore no magnetic boots here; gravity didn't care about him when you were down in the bowels. You had to walk. Or drag yourself.

His cyber-arm hung limp at his side, the coolant reservoir empty, leaking a slow, steady drip that sizzled on the warm stone floor. It wasn't working right. The hum was gone, replaced by a hollow throb that vibrated in his skull. He hadn't felt it for months. Now, every step sent a jolt through his spine, a reminder of the debt he couldn't pay with credits anymore.

Behind him, the heavy footsteps echoed in the dark. Not his own. The Gut King's crew had found them. They weren't looking for Kael specifically; they were looking for anything worth taking. Rex was already gone, his parts scattered across the lower sectors like fallen fruit in a storm. Jax was somewhere between a ghost and a server farm node, trading soul fragments for energy credits while he still breathed. And Kael? Kael was the only thing left that hadn't been optimized away yet.

"Inventory check," the Gut King's voice came over the open channel, distorted by static. It sounded like grinding gears mixed with wet tissue tearing apart. "We need warm meat. Fresh cuts."

Kael didn't stop. He kept his head down, eyes scanning the shadows for thermal spikes. The air tasted of burnt copper and ozone, a sharp, metallic sting that coated the back of his throat. He could feel them closing in—scavengers born from friendly fire stripping dead Ronin for parts before they could even die properly.

"Keep moving," Kael muttered into the comms, voice rough. "They're scanning for heat signatures. If you leave a trail, you become part of the cycle."

The response was immediate—a rapid-fire exchange of code snippets and inventory lists spoken in grunts. *Thermal spike detected. Priority: extraction.*

Kael kicked a rusted pipe from his path, sending debris skittering across the floor. He had to run, but his legs felt like they were made of lead wrapped in static. The addiction was still there, a phantom ache in his neural port where Rex's spinal fuse used to be. Blue light bled into his irises like acid cutting through meat, even though he wasn't high. Just tired. Broken.

He reached the junction point marked by a flickering holographic billboard advertising synthetic protein bars that tasted like ash. The Dancehall Rhythm pulsed through the ground beneath them, a low-frequency vibration created by the collision of high-speed trains and the hum of holographic billboards syncing to a chaotic beat. It repelled law enforcement drones, but tonight, it was different. Different and dangerous.

The rhythm was wrong. The beat stuttered, skipping a bar as if someone had reached into the music box and pulled a wire. Kael's cyber-eye flickered, catching a reflection in the cracked glass of a vending machine. A faceless figure stood there, holding a tablet displaying a live feed of Sector Seven.

"Game over," the voice said. Not Johnson's smooth corporate lingo now. This was guttural, raw. "You ran the numbers wrong, scrap."

Kael didn't know who this was. Was it the Gut King? Or someone else inside Johnson's network trying to finish the job? The distinction didn't matter. The outcome was the same.

He activated the rhythm in his mind, forcing the city's noise into a chaotic loop. It was risky. If he messed up the timing, the drones would lock onto his biometric signature and incinerate him. But if he got it right, maybe he could create a momentary silence, a window of opportunity to reach the Spire one more time.

The first drone hit the beat with a mechanical screech, its sensors trying to parse the pattern. Kael jammed his arm into the air, firing a single pulse from the remaining battery in his wrist cannon. It struck the drone's midsection, sending sparks flying as the metal sheared apart.

"Nice shot," the Gut King growled, voice distorted by static. "But you forgot to eat."

Kael ignored him. He focused on the rhythm, letting the music override his fear. The drones swarmed closer, their red eyes glowing like angry beetles in the dark. They weren't just hunting; they were feeding on the chaos. Every movement he made consumed more of his life force, draining his cyber-parts faster.

One drone hovered directly above him, its thrusters humming aggressively. It fired a cluster of micro-missiles aimed at his head. Kael rolled sideways, the impact sending him skidding across the wet pavement. He gritted his teeth as pain shot through his spine, a sharp warning that he wasn't moving fast enough.

He caught himself on a pile of scrap metal, scraping his shoulder against rusted steel. Blood mixed with rain and oil, dripping down his arm. His cyber-arm was gone now, the casing crushed under the impact. The metal groaned, sparks showering around him as the circuitry failed.

"Inventory update," the Gut King's voice crackled again. "One Ronin less. Two Vultures more."

Kael coughed, spitting blood onto the ground. He looked down at his arm, then up at the Spire looming in the distance. It was dark now, the neon signs flickering out one by one as the city's power grid collapsed into a blackout zone.

"Can you hear me?" he shouted over the roar of the engines. "Do you still have your own name?"

The comms crackled, and for a split second, Johnson's voice cut through the noise. *"You're already dead. The only question is whether you'll remember it tomorrow."*

Then silence. Just static and the hum of the city eating him alive one byte at a time.

Kael pushed himself up, leaning against a crumbling wall. His vision blurred, colors washing over him in streaks of blue and gray. He wasn't sure if he was running anymore, or just being dragged along by the current of the Grid.

Behind him, more Vultures emerged from the shadows, their mechanical teeth clicking as they scanned for targets. The Gut King stood at the head of the pack, his body partially replaced with scrap metal, eyes replaced with lenses that scanned for movement rather than sight.

"Come and get them," he commanded, his voice rising over the hum of the city's defenses. "But know this: nothing is wasted here. Every part has a value, even if it's just fuel for another scavenger drone."

Kael stared at the Gut King, then down at the ruined arm in his hand. He realized something now. The rhythm wasn't supposed to protect him. It was supposed to erase him. The Grid used chaos as a weapon, turning survival into a glitch.

He took a step forward, toward the Spire, but his feet didn't move right. They felt heavy, anchored by invisible chains of code and debt. He tried to recall why he had come here in the first place, what Rex had said before they were taken apart, but the memory was slippery, like trying to hold water in a cup with holes in it.

"Where are you going?" the Gut King asked, stepping into his path. His mechanical claws scraped against the metal floor, leaving grooves in the stone. "There's no exit. Only receipts."

Kael didn't answer. He just kept walking, dragging himself through the dark corridors of the Gutter, following the faint trail of static leading back to the Core Chamber. The dancehall rhythm faded behind him, replaced by the sound of his own breathing, ragged and shallow.

He reached the threshold of the Spire's inner gate, a heavy steel door reinforced with data locks. He pressed his palm against the scanner, but there was no response. Just a flat line on the readout. Uncompiled.

The gates didn't open. Instead, they dissolved into pixels of light and shadow, revealing the core chamber beyond. Kael stumbled forward, falling onto the floor where server racks lined the walls, towering over him like ancient cathedrals made of burnt plastic and hot copper wire. Thousands of scanning cameras watched from the ceiling, their lenses blinking in unison.

He was alone again. Or at least, he thought he was.

But then Johnson appeared, standing in the center of the room, wearing casual clothes rather than his expensive suit. His hands were covered in blood that looked like oil spilled on pavement, dripping down his fingers onto the metal floor. The smell of ozone and burnt plastic hit him harder than before.

"You ran fast," Johnson said, voice smooth despite the mess on his hands. "Faster than the market allowed."

Kael stood up, wincing as pain tore through his body. His cyber-parts were failing, degrading faster than expected. He could feel the synapses dying off, one by one, like a circuit board being slowly eaten away by corrosion.

"What do you want?" Kael asked, voice trembling. "Is this it? The final line item?"

Johnson smiled without showing teeth, the skin tight around lips that hadn't moved since he bought them off. "I don't want anything from you, Kael. I already own you. The question is whether you'll remember it tomorrow."

He gestured to the screens on the walls, which were scrolling with lines of code and names. *"Receipt printed,"* Johnson said. *"No refunds."*

Kael looked at the screens, then down at his hands. They were shaking now, the veins pulsing with black fluid instead of blood. He realized then that he hadn't just been hunted; he had been bought back. Johnson had offered him a choice: servitude or liquidation. And Kael had chosen neither.

He looked at the Spire, the needle in the neon jungle guarded by cameras watching more than recording. Inside, gravity was adjusted by magnetic boots; you floated slightly unless you engaged the floor plates. But there were no magnetic boots left for him to use. Just static.

Kael closed his eyes and let the noise take him. The Grid was ready to erase him, and for the first time in years, he didn't fight it. Not because he was afraid. Because he finally understood that fear wasn't real either. It was just another line item on a contract he couldn't sign anymore.

Outside, the wind picked up again, swirling dust and debris into a vortex that threatened to swallow the Spire whole. The rain hammered against the black glass, a rhythmic punctuation mark to the screaming servers that lined the walls.

Somewhere in the distance, a mechanical growl rose from the sewer tunnels, low enough to vibrate in his bones. The Gut King's crew was already moving, scavengers born from friendly fire stripping dead Ronin for parts before they could even die properly. They weren't looking for Kael specifically; they were looking for anything worth taking.

But Kael didn't care about the Vultures anymore. He cared about the silence. The quiet that followed a sound that was never meant to be heard by anyone but the machine itself.

He took one last step toward the center of the Core Chamber, where the server racks pulsed with an unnatural light. Johnson watched him, hands still stained with oil and blood, waiting for the final transaction to complete.

Kael placed his palm on the mainframe console, fingers trembling as he pressed the button labeled *FINALIZE*. The screen flashed white, then black.

The hum of the servers stopped. The cameras went dark. Even the rain outside seemed to cease its hammering against the glass. For a moment, nothing moved. Nothing existed.

Then Johnson spoke again, softer this time, almost conversational. "Beautiful and terrible all at once."

Kael's face glowed on the neon sign outside, flickering briefly before fading into static. The camera panned up to the Spire, which was now dark. A single line of text appeared on the screen in front of him: *ACCEPTED.*

Receipt printed. No refunds.
