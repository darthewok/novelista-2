### Trial 1 — Hard-Boiled Noir
The rain on Sector Seven didn't wash the grime; it just made it slicker. Kael adjusted his retinal implant, the blue light bleeding into his cornea like acid. He saw the contract: zero dollars, negative risk, a faceless voice that sounded like grinding stone eating bone. *Erase him,* the command read. *No right to life.*

"Copy that," Kael whispered. His throat felt dry, tasting of copper and cheap energy drink. He was part of the Ronin now, a crew of ghosts with cyber-arms and spinal ports plugged into holo-tables that screamed prices they couldn't afford. The Spire loomed ahead, a needle in the neon jungle, guarded by cameras that watched more than they recorded.

Inside the tower, the client—Mr. Johnson himself—dressed in a suit that cost more than Kael's entire life savings. He smiled without showing teeth. "You have your work cut out," he said, though it sounded like he'd already heard his endgame. "The final line item includes... us."

Kael's heart did not pound. His hands shook. The digital dopamine hit him like a freight train of synthetic joy, a glitch in the matrix that made his brain short-circuit with euphoria. He ran into the rain to hide, but the gutters were full of Vultures now—scavengers born from friendly fire, stripping dead Ronin for parts before they could even die properly.

"Where are you?" Kael shouted over the hum of neon signs flickering and dying. "I'm not gonna let you do this!" The words caught in his throat. He was sold out, and he wasn't asking why anymore. Just wanted to run. But the rain had turned him into a target. And targets were hunted. Fast.

A shadow moved behind him. A figure stepped from the mist, eyes glowing red with retinal scans. "Receipt printed," the man said, voice like static. "No refunds." Kael reached for his cyber-weapon, but it felt heavy, dead inside him. The euphoria was fading, replaced by the cold realization: loyalty is a commodity bought and sold in this city. And tonight, he was just the hardware in someone else's machine.

The streetlights hummed as sirens wailed in the distance. Someone had found them. All of them. They were not alone anymore. No, they never were. Not when you're part of the Ronin. Just another name on a list waiting to be erased. The world spun faster than Kael's heart could keep up with. There was no time left to think about what came next. Only the rain and the noise of dying electronics around him.

He looked down at his palm where a pulse of data streamed across his skin—alive, dead, or just code? He didn't know anymore. The city ate those who asked questions. And tonight, he was food on the menu.

### Trial 2 — Fractured Tech
Static screamed through Kael's comms, overlaying the rain with frequencies only a neural-addict could hear. *Chrome Trigger,* his thoughts echoed, looping like bad code. The Ronin stood in Sector Seven's market, neon bleeding into wet asphalt. He wasn't alone; there were four others, all hollowed out by contracts they couldn't refuse.

"Contract confirmed," Kael said, his voice glitching as the dopamine hit him hard, a digital overdose disguised as a mission. "Zero risk. Zero payout?" No, not zero payout. A lifetime subscription to hell. The Spire loomed ahead, black monolith against a sky full of holographic ads selling organs they didn't need.

The target was Mr. Johnson, tower exec, or maybe ghost in the machine? "He wants his rival erased," Kael muttered, adjusting his cyber-arms which whined as he powered up. Too many ports exposed. Too much heat. The rain slicked his skin, making him feel slippery, useless.

Suddenly, Vultures emerged from the alleyway, scavengers born in the ash of friendly fire. Their leader laughed, a sound like broken glass grinding against stone. "Too slow, Ronin." He raised a weapon that looked like a cocktail shaker made of light. "You're not worth the cleanup fee."

Kael didn't flinch. Not yet. His eyes burned with synthetic joy, drowning out the warning bells ringing in his skull. *Dopamine loop,* he thought. *Too deep.* The euphoria masked the truth: they were all set to be erased as the final line item in Mr. Johnson's contract. Loyalty was a commodity bought at a discount. Bodies were hardware, replaceable parts in someone else's game.

A figure stepped forward, face obscured by shadow and static. "Receipt printed," he whispered. No refunds allowed. The crew realized then what they'd done: they'd sold themselves into a trap armed with nothing but fake bravado and too much digital sugar.

The rain continued to fall, washing away evidence as the Vultures began their work. Strip dead Ronin for parts. Leave the meat for the rats. Kael watched his comrades fade into smoke, each one a glitch in the system. One of them looked directly at him, mouth moving silently. *Sold,* the thought echoed, louder than the sirens wailing in the distance.

He tried to scream, but his voice failed. Just another line item on a bill no one would pay. The city spun faster than Kael's mind could process. There was no time left to question the math. Only the noise and the rain, relentless as judgment. And somewhere beneath the surface, the code whispered back: *Waste not. Want not.*

"Target acquired," Kael yelled into his comms, voice cracking under the weight of digital dopamine flooding his system. Too much. The mission was simple: erase Mr. Johnson's rival inside the Spire. Easy money. Or so the contract said. Until the final line item revealed itself: us too.
