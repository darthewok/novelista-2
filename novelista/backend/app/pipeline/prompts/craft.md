# Craft Reference (compact)

## Worldbuilding (Sanderson-informed, genre-neutral)
- **First Law:** The plot may only be resolved by rules the reader already understands. Magic or technology used to solve problems must be *established* before they're *used*.
- **Second Law:** Limitations are more interesting than powers. What can a character *not* do? What does a power *cost*?
- **Third Law:** Expand what you have before adding something new. Compound existing mechanics rather than stacking fresh ones.
- **Interconnection:** Magic shapes economy shapes politics shapes culture. Any concept named must ripple into at least two other systems.

## Character
- **Wound / Want / Need / Lie:** a living character has a past wound, a surface want, a deeper need, and a lie they tell themselves that blocks the need.
- **Speech distinctiveness:** each POV/major character should be identifiable from 3 lines of dialogue alone — rhythm, vocabulary, topic, filler, metaphor-class all vary.
- **Three Sliders:** Competence / Likability / Proactivity. Pick two to max per character; the third is how they grow.

## Plot
- **Try-Fail Cycles:** protagonists should fail forward; each attempt should *complicate* the situation, not merely delay.
- **Save the Cat beats** (for novels): Opening Image, Theme Stated, Setup, Catalyst, Debate, Break into Two, B Story, Fun & Games, Midpoint, Bad Guys Close In, All Is Lost, Dark Night of the Soul, Break into Three, Finale, Final Image.
- **Plant & Payoff:** significant payoffs need *two* plants minimum — one overt, one subtle. Keep a foreshadowing ledger.

## Prose rules (anti-AI-slop)
- **Body-first emotion.** "Her throat tightened" before "She felt scared."
- **Cut filter verbs** when they dull perception: *felt*, *seemed*, *noticed*, *realized*, *watched as*.
- **No throat-clearing.** Openings enter the scene already in motion.
- **Specific over generic.** Not "a tree" — "a birch, bark split like old paper."
- **Avoid abstractions as subjects.** "Hope surged" is weaker than an action that *shows* hope.
