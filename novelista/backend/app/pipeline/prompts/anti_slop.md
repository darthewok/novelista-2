# Anti-Slop Reference

## Tier 1 — Banned phrases (reject on sight)
- "a testament to"
- "in the realm of"
- "it is worth noting"
- "delve into"
- "nestled amidst"
- "tapestry of"
- "navigate the complexities"
- "stood as a testament"
- "sent shivers"
- "her heart raced" / "his heart pounded" (without reason)
- "a mixture of [A] and [B]" (describing emotion)
- "couldn't help but"
- "a shiver ran down"

## Tier 2 — Overused words (use < once per chapter)
- palpable, ethereal, cacophony, myriad, plethora, vibrant, serene, rustic,
  bustling, pristine, quaint, whimsical, tangible, intricate, profound, timeless.

## Tier 3 — AI tells
- Endings that restate the theme in plain language.
- Over-explained metaphors ("X was like Y, because Z.").
- Characters describing their own emotions in narration.
- Paragraphs that start with "In conclusion," "Indeed," or "Moreover."
- Rhetorical questions the narrator answers immediately.
- Perfect parallelism in prose (three-beat rule: vary cadence).

## Show-don't-tell checklist
- Is any emotion named directly (angry, sad, happy)? Replace with body or action.
- Any filter verb (saw/heard/felt/noticed/realized)? Usually remove.
- Does dialogue "as you know"-explain? Cut the explanation; trust the reader.
